Does the Holy Life ever feel like it's just "over there?"

You can see it, but it's out of reach. You already know how to attain it, but right now you're stuck. It's just over there. If only you could reach a little further.

You thought you were doing well, walking with God.

You start to feel you don't deserve it, like it was meant for others but not for you. A voice - an emotion - arises that almost whispers "this is not for you. You don't deserve this." The light within you seems to dim...

I've been there.

I recognize it as the work of Satan tempting me to doubt God. Thanks to the Sacred Word and Tradition, I'm better equipped to deal with it now and I want to share with you what works for me.
In The Moment

The very first thing I do when I recognize I am under spiritual attack is to invoke God's protection through St. Michael:

> St. Michael The Archangel
> defend us in battle
> Be our defense against the wickedness and snares
> of the devil.
> May God rebuke him
> we humbly pray
> And do thou, o Prince of The Heavenly Hosts,
> cast into Hell Satan,
> and the other evil spirits,
> who prowl about the world
> seeking the ruin of souls.
> Amen.

I then re-affirm my faith and allegiance with The Lord's Prayer:

> Our Father
> who art in Heaven,
> Hallowed be Thy name.
> Thy kingdom come,
> Thy will be done,
> on Earth as it is in Heaven.
> Give us this day our daily bread
> and forgive us our trespasses
> as we forgive those who trespass against us
> And lead us not into temptation,
> but deliver us from evil.
> Amen.

This immediately helps, but sometimes I repeat these prayers a few times.

Once the threat has passed, I think about what's going on. How long did I have these unwanted thoughts and emotions? How do I stop them from recurring?
Long Term Support

To avoid this temptation, I turn to Scripture and constant [ Prayer ][prayer].
Any time of day, a silent prayer is like manna in the desert for a thirsting
soul.

The [ Bible in a Year ][biy] podcast has bolstered my faith. Hearing the Word
proclaimed has unlocked my comprehension and recall more than reading ever has.
Passages like [ James 4:7 ][james47]

> 7 Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from
> you.

offer more comfort and solace when they are understood in context and relationship to the rest of the Bible.

As a reader (or listener!) receiving St. Paul's warning to the budding church in Ephesus against temptation (e.g., [ Ephesians 4:26-27 ][ephe4]), it feels as though God is speaking directly to me through Paul.
