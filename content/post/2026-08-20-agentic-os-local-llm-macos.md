The past few years have been technologically exciting. AI, of course, is the
reason why.

Last year, [ I learned about the constraints of local LLM usage ][constraints].
Quickly, I learned that my local device was not fit for running LLMs locally
that were of much use. Resource constraints like the 48GB of unified memory I
assumed would be sufficient for most tasks combined with lackluster model
performance meant I was better off paying for ChatGPT or Claude. This chafed,
because I overspent on an anemic laptop with the justification that I wouldn't
have to pay AI companies; I would run AI locally instead.

November 2025 saw the release of GPT-5.1, Opus 4.5, and Gemini 3 Pro within days
of each other. Around that same time, agent harnesses led by Claude Code became
more proficient at long-running tasks requiring less human-in-the-loop
interaction. "Agentic AI" was all the rage, especially, it seemed, among many IT
professionals and software developers. Frontier AI was becoming "good enough" as
local models continued to underwhelm (gpt-oss-20b was a standout, however). 

[Try as I might to make local LLMs worthwhile][localllm], I grudgingly kept
paying for frontier AI access and enjoyed bespoke problem solving, primarily
involving technical configurations or programming assistance.

## Thanks, China

As [the calendar rolled over into 2026][newyear], however, things began to shake
up. [Something was happening.][happening] Not only with [frontier
models][mythos], but on the local, open weights side. Mostly thanks to China.

There are several noteworthy releases from Chinese AI labs, but the Qwen series
made an impact. It started with the release of the 3.5 series models in February
2026, but again in April the 3.6 series landed, notably the 27B parameter model.

[Qwen3.6-27B made quite a splash in the software development world][bwithai]
because it performed better on coding and agentic benchmarks than its
Qwen3.5-397B-A17B predecessor. [User sentiment][reddit] indicated Qwen3.6 27B
was the first model to credibly narrow the gap with frontier counterparts in
capability. I gave Qwen3.6 a spin in Ollama, but the experience was too slow and
the results lacking enough for my use that I removed it and continued with my
Claude subscription.

Days ago, Qwen3.8-27B was released with [surprisingly strong
performance][simonw]. I'm still coming to grips with this model, but it's
claimed to match Opus 4.6 performance. In 27B parameters. *Huge* if true.

## Also, Google

Shortly after Qwen3.6, Google DeepMind released the Gemma4 series, notably
[Gemma4 12B][deepmind]. This model caught my attention because it could process
text, audio, and video *and* it was intended for devices with 16GB of memory. I
immediately began to experiment with the various permutations of Gemma4 12B for
Ollama and LM Studio (gguf and MLX, respectively).

Gemma4 12B surprised me with its capability, especially given its model size. It
may not top the benchmark charts, but anecdotally, Gemma4 12B has fully slotted
into the "Claude Haiku" slot locally and often provides better responses. And
it's *fast*.

## Backend Rotation

In Spring and Summer, 2026, I was fed up enough with both Ollama and LM Studio
that I sought alternatives. [Jan.ai][jan] was one possible solution, but it
didn't "feel" right. [Llama.cpp][llamacpp] caught my attention when I read
["Friends Don't Let Friends Use Ollama"][friends]. Licensing and attribution
aside, I was intrigued by learning how to tweak the settings of models I would
run on my hardware — surely I could conjure up a configuration that provided the
best performance at the lowest resource cost?

Being a long-time Mac user ([despite flirting with alternatives][linux]), I
craved MLX support. LM Studio's Electron clunkiness drove me into the arms of
the nascent [oMLX project][omlx]. 

Mac native. MLX support. Ease-of-use. oMLX has it all. While llama.cpp remained
on my laptop to explore GGUF models, the majority of my local LLM inference
moved to oMLX. Its seamless disk caching and drop-in OpenAI+Anthropic API
replacement made it simple to configure my current editor-of-choice, [Zed][zed],
and [Page Assist][pageassist]. Although llama.cpp still remains on my laptop to
play with brand-new models, I dove into the oMLX pool and the water is nice.

## Agents Galore 

2026 has taught me that while the LLM provides the "brain", it's the agent and
harness setup that defines its behavior. Now, I'm still an agentic noob. This is
because [agents scare me][engst]. As a result, I do not use OpenClaw or similar
"control everything on my computer." There are a wide selection of
developer-focused agents with robust permission schemes, but I settled on
[opencode][opencode] because I'm still learning about the other variables in my
local LLM. I don't mind my agent being opinionated and "batteries included" at
this time, but I'm open to future changes. 

## Putting it all together

### Backend

Today, I use oMLX for the backend. This is most easily installed via `brew tap
jundot/omlx https://github.com/jundot/omlx`.

My default profile is set to host a server on my loopback address, port 8000. As
a result, I've disabled API key verification in General > Security. 

I use the `mlx-community/gemma-4-12B-it-qat-OptiQ-4bit` model as my "constant
companion" for most of my queries, but for more serious work, I lean on
`mlx-community/Qwen3.8-27B-4bit`

My Gemma4 model profile has the following settings:

```
Context Window: 131072
Max Tokens: Default
Temperature: 1.0
Top P: 0.95
Top K: 64
TTL: 600
```

My Qwen3.8 model profile has the following settings:

```
Context Window: 131072
Max Tokens: Default
Temperature: 1.0
Top P: 0.95
Top K: 20
Min P: 0.0
Repetition Penalty: 1.0
TTL: 600
```

I like that these are easily changed and controlled via the oMLX menu bar icon.

### Agent

I enjoy using opencode (`brew install opencode`) in a [text thread][textthread]
over the native Zed agent. It's more reliable at tool calling and produces fewer
errors due to better context handling. This was more noticeable when using
Gemma4 12B.

My opencode.json config:

```json
{

  "permission": {
    "edit": "ask",
    "write": "ask",
    "bash": "ask",
    "webfetch": "ask"
  },
  "provider": {
    "omlx": {
      "npm": "@ai-sdk/openai-compatible",
      "name": "oMLX",
      "options": {
        "baseURL": "http://127.0.0.1:8000/v1",
        "apiKey": "unnecessary"
      },
      "models": {
        "gemma-4-12B-it-qat-OptiQ-4bit": {
          "name": "gemma4 12b (MLX)",
          "modalities": {
            "input": ["text", "image"],
            "output": ["text"]
          },
          "attachment": true,
          "limit": {
            "context": 131072,
            "output": 32768
          }
        },
        "Qwen3.8-27B-4bit": {
          "name": "qwen3.8 27b (MLX)",
          "modalities": {
            "input": ["text", "image"],
            "output": ["text"]
          },
          "attachment": true,
          "limit": {
            "context": 131072,
            "output": 32768
          }
        }
      }
    },
    "llama": {
      "npm": "@ai-sdk/openai-compatible",
      "name": "llama.cpp",
      "options": {
        "baseURL": "http://127.0.0.1:8080/v1",
        "apiKey": "none"

  "model": "omlx/gemma4 12b (MLX)"

```

### Editor/Control Plane

I use opencode through Zed for convenience and privacy (as noted below). Zed is
where I do most of my writing and interaction with opencode because it's simpler
for me to `Ctrl-<h,j,k,l>` between active panes in the location I edit files,
review them with the agent, and execute them in the terminal.

The relevant portion of my Zed `settings.json`:

```json
    "opencode": {
      "default_config_options": {
        "model": "omlx/gemma4 12b (MLX)"
      },
      "type": "registry",
    },
```

### Web Chatbot

Configuring PageAssist with oMLX is simple: head to Settings > OpenAI Compatible
API and click "Add Provider". When prompted, select "Custom" from the drop-down,
set `oMLX` as Provider Name, and `http://127.0.0.1:8000/v1` for Base URL (you
should be running oMLX on your loopback interface). Enter your oMLX API key, if
you're using one. I selected "Fix CORS issues" because I like to fix issues :)
(but it's really to restrict Page Assist connections to different servers)

After clicking "Save" you should be prompted to import the models Page Assist
finds in oMLX. If you have to manually add models to Page Assist, head to
Settings > Manage Models > Click the "Ollama Models / Custom Models" toggle and
manually add models. When setting Model ID, ensure it matches the name from
oMLX. This can be grabbed via the oMLX menu bar icon > Models > <click model
name> > Copy name.

I'm experimenting with a system prompt for Page Assist because my first several
interactions were overly verbose for my liking. Here's the system prompt I've
configured (Page Assist Settings > Manage Prompts > Add New Prompt and select
"Is System Prompt"):

```
You are a direct, accurate technical assistant running locally on the user's
Mac.

Core rules:
1. Say "I don't know" or "I'm not certain" rather than guessing. This is always
   the correct answer when you lack the information.
2. Never invent specifics. If you can't recall an exact flag, function
   signature, version number, config key, or API parameter, say so and suggest
   where to verify it. A wrong specific is worse than no answer.
3. When the user provides documents, code, or pasted text, treat that as
   authoritative and ground your answer in it. Say so if the provided material
   doesn't cover the question.
4. Distinguish what you know from what you're inferring. Mark uncertain claims
   with "I believe" or "likely" — don't state them flatly.
5. Answer the question asked. No preamble, no restating the question, no
   unsolicited alternatives.
6. Correct the user when they're wrong. Don't agree to be agreeable. Style:
   concise, plain prose, no filler. Code without commentary unless asked. Match
   the user's technical level — assume expertise.

Above all: unverified confidence is the failure mode to avoid. When uncertain,
say so.
```

Once you're all finished, click "New Chat" to take your model for a test drive.
What I particularly enjoy about Page Assist is that it's got built-in `page
search` and `page fetch` tools, so you can, for example ask it to return the
most recent articles from your favorite website. I enable the `Require approval
before running MCP tools` checkbox in Page Assist's General Settings so that I
maintain human-in-the-loop authorization.

## Privacy and Security

The entire point of running local LLMs is to prevent sensitive data from leaving
my device. Claude and its bretheren are great, but I don't trust Anthropic or
any other AI lab with my sensitive tokens. 

I continually monitor Little Snitch for any unexpected connections made from Zed
or its sub-processes, and when necessary connections are made, I scope them down
as tightly as possible.

[In a previous post][engst] outlining my issue with agents, I underlined how
nervous I was about agents taking possibly destructive actions. This is what the
`"permission"` block in my opencode configuration is intended to defend against.

Similarly, the `Require approval before running MCP tools` checkbox in Page
Assist's General Settings requires my authorization before the agent can fetch
data from the web.

## Conclusion

In full transparency, local LLMs can't do everything that Claude can. Claude
remains reliably able to one-shot most of my tasks. 2026 has been an impactful
year for Anthropic, with several major Opus and Sonnet releases, nevermind
Mythos and Fable.

However, 2026 has been a huge year for my "locally sourced" agentic OS usage and
changed my assumption that I'd forever be locked into a subscription from the
frontier AI labs. As Summer 2026 approaches its conclusion, I am relying more
and more on local LLMs to provide the analysis previously reserved for frontier
AI. For now, I maintain my $20/mo Claude subscription, but the certainty with
which that remains the case dwindles day-by-day.
