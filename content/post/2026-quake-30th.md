Yesterday, [Bethesda released a major update][bethesda] for *Quake*—one of the
most influential games of my formative years, and video gaming as a whole.
Setting aside the nostalgia this event evokes (many of my cherished tween
memories involve *Quake*), I'm surprised and delighted by this.

I'm surprised because [there were recently massive layoffs at id
software][gamedeveloper] that left the company's future in jeopardy. This
shocked me: *"Surely, id Software wouldn't be gutted? Some of gaming's legendary
series came from id,"* I thought. Reading [the release statement at
Bethesda][bethesda] reveals they contracted the development of this update to
MachineGames, so even if the *Quake* product owners at id were impacted by the
layoffs, this release was almost certainly finished before they were let go.
Still, the fact that such massive layoffs hit one of gaming's most important and
influential design studios is concerning.

I'm delighted because it's great to see support for a *thirty-year-old game*
continue. Of course, this isn't just *any* tricenarian; *Quake* is one of the
first truly 3D PC FPS games whose impact reverberated throughout all of gaming
when it released in 1996. That a game of this vintage continues to receive
support and have new official content produced should be celebrated.

## Extensibility Equals Longevity

While new official content is welcome, *Quake* has continued to thrive in the
past three decades because of John Carmack's decision to release the game's
engine as open source along with level and asset editors. When id Software moved
on to *Quake II* and later projects, fans of the original—myself very much
included—could continue to modify (or "mod") *Quake* and extend its life. 

Those cherished tween memories I mentioned a moment ago involve countless hours
"mapping" or creating levels for *Quake*. My first software purchase was Ben
Morris's [*WorldCraft*][worldcraft], a level editor for *Quake* that allowed me
to bring my level ideas to life. Although I first started mapping id's *Doom*
and *Doom II*, it was *Quake* mapping that allowed me to extend my creativity
vertically through 3D worlds and levels.

And I wasn't the only one. The creators of *Team Fortress*, a popular *Quake*
mod, went on to be hired by Valve Software to develop *Team Fortress Classic*
for Half-Life and then eventually *Team Fortress 2,* a game with [~60k daily
active players][steamcharts].

If id Software had kept *Quake* closed-source without releasing development
tools, I doubt it would have survived as a cultural icon.

## Longevity Equals Creativity

Beyond cultural relevance, id's openness enabled an avalanche of excellent
content for *Quake*. I've only moderately followed the *Quake* modding scene
since the 2000s, but here are just some of the *excellent* mods you can play for
free:

- [Arcane Dimensions][arcane] (my favorite so far: new weapons and monsters, and
  some individual maps are as long as entire vanilla *Quake* episodes!)
- [Alkaline Quake][alkaline]
- [Quake Brutalist Jam 3][qbj3]
- [More at Slipgate Sightseer][slipseer]

## Celebration

And so, today when work is over, I'll be doing what I did thirty years ago:
firing up *Quake* and talking about it on IRC. Here's to thirty more years!
