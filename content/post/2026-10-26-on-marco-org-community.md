Marco Arment [recently shared an honest post][marco] that struck a chord with me
for two reasons.

First, the loneliness he shares is palpable. It's immediately recognizable to
anyone interested in niche subjects. For me, programming and general computer
nerdery were niche growing up in the '90s — or at least it seemed that way
based on the community I grew up in. It turns out I wasn't alone, but I digress.
Family, friends, and community are hugely important, but there's something
special about participating in a group of like-minded people in pursuit of one's
interests.

In this, I share Marco's loneliness.

Second — and what prompted me to write this — is the treatment Marco gives to
the notion of changing one's mind and recognizing the need for tolerance. He
doesn't use that term in his article, but my read is that he's justifying the
exploration of previously "forbidden" places in search of like-minded
individuals.

Setting aside Marco specifically, this is an incredibly important acknowledgment
for this time and place. I suspect many would agree that polarization and
tribalism have swelled to undesired proportions in the past several years. I
think it's precisely *because* a vocal minority has chastised and oppressed the
majority, shaming anyone who dares explore a "forbidden" topic, that the
situation has become this untenable.

I quote "forbidden" because I'm old enough to remember a time when people, at
least in the West, were free to disagree on topics, and that didn't prevent them
from living, working, and celebrating together. Believe it or not, life was more
tolerant back then, before "tolerance" was the zeitgeist.

And so, I celebrate Marco's post because, to me, it represents an effort to
return to a better time. I think we might all be less lonely if we could agree
to disagree.
