This morning, I saw ["Hang on to Your Firefox"][hangon] on HackerNews. I had to
read it because Firefox is an emotional anchor for me: I've been using it
(non-continuously) since 2004, more than half of my life. It hasn't always been
my default browser because others have cropped up over time, some offering
compelling unique features that Firefox hadn't implemented. 

But, whether through familiarity or merit, I always find myself coming back to
it. As ["Hang on to Your Firefox"][hangon] points out, and [as I've
commented][mytake], browser engine diversity is important: without it, users
will not have the choice that aligns with their preferences for security,
privacy, or innovation. 

I choose to use Firefox because it is a small vote against the monopolization of
the web. Mozilla isn't perfect, and neither is Firefox. I disagree with their
involvement in political activities and some [questionable][pcmag]
[activities][mrrobot], but I more strongly disagree with what their competition
[is doing][futurism] or [has done][wiki].

Without Mozilla supporting Firefox, there would be a de facto duopoly between
Google—who wants to be the intermediary between users and search, browsing,
and advertising—and Apple—who wants to be the intermediary between users and
the web platform. Google is incentivized to keep users within its ecosystem of
web properties and Apple is incentivized to keep users off the web entirely and
within its ecosystem of apps.

And as the author of ["Hang on to Your Firefox"][hangon] predicted, the [HN
discussion][hn] has focused on anecdata around how "Firefox used to be amazing
but now sucks." 

If this is you, I humbly submit that you should give Firefox another shot,
especially if you haven't used it in 2026. [I wrote in March][ffcooking] about
how Firefox had introduced many desirable features this year. Since then,
they've continued apace to the point that I no longer feel the need to explore
alternatives because it has what I want.

If you're a web developer, please continue to test your applications against
Firefox as well as Chrome. The web needs you to. Users need you to.
