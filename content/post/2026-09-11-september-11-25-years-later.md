I'm not sure what amazes me more: that it's been a *quarter century* since the
September 11 terror attacks, or that today still evokes the same well of
emotions. Though blunted by time somewhat, they remain visceral:

The sudden shock as I was awoken by my father, who curtly said "Come downstairs.
We're under attack."

The confusion as I struggled to process the visual of United Airlines Flight 175
crashing into the South Tower of the World Trade Center on live television.

The fear of not knowing whether my mother was safe: she was commuting up the 395
corridor in Arlington, very close to the Pentagon. The anxiety increased as the
mobile phone lines were completely saturated with people checking on their loved
ones.

The horror of watching hapless victims leaping to their deaths on television to
avoid being burned alive.

The witches' brew of feelings churning in my bosom as I was glued to the
television that day.

Although these events occurred more than half of my life ago, they were pivotal
to the development of who I've become. With age, I've come to realize how
historic events only carry their full weight if experienced in real time. My
children learn about and discuss September 11 in school, but they don't feel it.

Perhaps that's for the best.

May God bless those 2,977 who died that day. May He bless all those who have
died in conflicts and terror attacks. And may we do everything we can as a
nation to prevent such an atrocity from occurring again—here or elsewhere.
